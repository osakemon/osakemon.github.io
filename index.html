Weiqing Chen

Machine Learning Researcher · Multimodal AI & Computational Biology

Weill Cornell Medicine & Houston Methodist, Houston, Texas, United States

I build multimodal foundation models and research tools that connect tissue morphology with molecular measurements, and release them as open-source software.

I am a fifth-year PhD candidate in Physiology, Biophysics and Systems Biology at Weill Cornell Medicine and a visiting graduate research fellow at Houston Methodist Research Institute, advised by Dr. Guangyu Wang. I expect to complete my PhD in early 2027. My research spans computational pathology, single-cell and spatial biology, multi-omics integration, and dynamical modeling of biological systems.

I led OmiCLIP, a visual–omics foundation model, and its platform Loki end to end: model design, data curation at the scale of millions of samples, training and evaluation, downstream applications, and open-source release with documentation and tutorials. My first-author manuscript on a single-cell pathology foundation model for molecular decoding of H&E images is under revision at Nature. Along the way I have mentored five junior PhD students, rotation students and research assistants.

My background is international and interdisciplinary: computer science in China, theoretical biology in Japan, and AI for biomedicine in the United States. Before my PhD I developed a Bayesian active-inference framework for goal-directed cell migration with Dr. Naoki Honda during my master’s at Kyoto University, and I hold a bachelor’s degree in Computer Science and Technology from Nanjing University of Posts and Telecommunications.

Open to industry research roles as a machine learning researcher or research scientist in multimodal AI and computational biology. Available early 2027; based in Houston, TX and open to relocation.

Nature Methods, 2025 · First author

OmiCLIP & Loki: connecting tissue images and gene expression

A visual–omics foundation model that learns a shared representation of H&E histology and transcriptomics through contrastive learning. The Loki toolkit uses it for tissue alignment, annotation, cell-type decomposition, image–transcriptomics retrieval and gene expression prediction from H&E alone.

  • 2.2M paired image–omics profiles
  • 1,007 tissue samples
  • 32 organs
  • 22 comparison methods

Nature Communications, 2025 · Co-first author

Thor & Mjolnir: spatial transcriptomics and histology at cell resolution

Thor infers single-cell spatial transcriptomes from spot-level data with an anti-shrinking Markov diffusion over a cell graph built from histology and gene expression, and ships 10 modular analysis tools. Mjolnir, its web interface, puts the results on the full-resolution slide. Applications include CNV-based tumor mapping in ductal carcinoma in situ and regenerative signatures in heart failure.

Nature Biotechnology, 2024 · Co-author

cellDancer: modeling RNA velocity and cell-state dynamics

A deep learning framework that infers cell-specific RNA velocity from single-cell sequencing data. By estimating transcription, splicing, and degradation rates for individual cells, cellDancer models heterogeneous cell-state transitions across developmental stages and lineages.
