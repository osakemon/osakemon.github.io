Weiqing Chen

AI for Science · Multimodal Learning · Computational Biology

Weill Cornell Medicine & Houston Methodist, Houston, Texas, United States

I develop multimodal foundation models and research tools for biological discovery, connecting tissue morphology with molecular measurements.

I am a fifth-year PhD candidate at Weill Cornell Medicine and a visiting graduate research fellow at Houston Methodist, advised by Dr. Guangyu Wang. My research spans computational pathology, single-cell and spatial biology, multi-omics integration, and dynamical modeling of biological systems.

I work across model design, data curation, training, evaluation, downstream applications, and software release. My work combines foundation models, multimodal representation learning, and contrastive learning with analyses of transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomic data to study biology and disease.

Previously, I studied Active Inference for goal-directed migration with Dr. Naoki Honda during my master’s at Kyoto University. I hold a bachelor’s degree in Computer Science and Technology from Nanjing University of Posts and Telecommunications.

Open to industry research opportunities in AI for Science and biomedical AI

Nature Methods, 2025 · Co-first author

OmiCLIP & Loki: connecting tissue images and gene expression

A visual–omics foundation model that learns a shared representation of histology and transcriptomics through contrastive learning. The Loki platform uses these representations for retrieval, tissue annotation, alignment, cell-type decomposition, and gene expression prediction.

  • 2.2M paired image–omics profiles
  • 1,007 tissue samples
  • 32 organs
  • 22 comparison methods

Nature Communications, 2025 · Co-first author

Thor: integrating spatial transcriptomics and histology at cell resolution

Integrates histology and spatial transcriptomics at cellular resolution using graph diffusion. Applications include CNV-based tumor mapping in ductal carcinoma in situ and analysis of vascular PLA2G2A enrichment in heart tissue following LVAD support.

Paper → Code

Nature Biotechnology, 2024 · Co-author

cellDancer: modeling RNA velocity and cell-state dynamics

A deep learning framework that infers cell-specific RNA velocity from single-cell sequencing data. By estimating transcription, splicing, and degradation rates for individual cells, cellDancer models heterogeneous cell-state transitions across developmental stages and lineages.
